# Missing Monday — three-minute demo

Target: about 2:50, including short pauses. Speak naturally. Let the screen carry the part numbers; explain why each step matters. The opening tells Clemens’s personal experience at Mercedes-Benz. The product demonstration that follows uses a fictional company and orders. The previous personal-story draft is preserved in .local-backup/demo-script-before-nov1-clarity.md.

## 0:00–0:30 · The Monday that inspired it

**Show:** The opening screen while telling the story. Move into the fictional practice order as the story ends.

**Say:**
“When I worked at Mercedes-Benz, my colleague Morgan was home sick one Monday. I had to cover her work, including a customer order she was handling.

We had a handbook, but this order was an exception. I couldn’t find the answer, and my other colleagues didn’t know either. The work was there. The knowledge we needed wasn’t.

I’m Clemens. That Monday inspired Missing Monday: a way to find those knowledge gaps before someone is away.”

## 0:30–0:50 · Find the missing knowledge

**Show:** Start S01. Open the order details and handbook, then choose **Help me find what’s missing**. Show the actual question returned.

**Say:**
“Let me show you with a fictional purchasing team. Morgan is the experienced buyer. Alex is covering.

A supplier offers a replacement part. The handbook says to check the approved rule—but that rule is missing.

A Meta model reads the available information and turns the blockage into one focused question for Morgan.”

## 0:50–1:15 · Turn the answer into a handover

**Show:** Enter the prepared fictional expert answer. Choose **Draft a handover note**, review the wording, then **Approve this note**. Keep the approval visible.

**Say:**
“Morgan explains when the replacement is allowed, which certificate is required, and when Alex must stop.

The AI drafts a short note and quotes Morgan’s answer. Morgan checks and approves the exact wording before it becomes available on a new order.

The AI can draft the advice. It cannot approve its own work.”

## 1:15–1:45 · Check that someone else can use it

**Show:** Choose **Try a new order · S02**. Open the order and approved note. Ask for a suggested next step, review it and save the actual response.

**Say:**
“Now a different order. Morgan is no longer helping. Alex uses the approved note to make the next decision.

The exercise checks that decision against a separate answer key the AI cannot see. Here, the decision is correct without another request to the expert.

We have evidence that the handover works on this order.”

## 1:45–2:10 · Check where the advice stops

**Show:** Choose **Check a harder order · S05**. Open its details and the approved note. Show and save the actual recommendation. Use the following narration only if the real response rejects the order.

**Say:**
“But a handover must also prevent a mistake. This order has a different certificate. The note does not allow it, and the order is rejected.

Missing Monday also lets you test the note with AI-generated harder orders. If the advice falls short, the failure stays visible and a correction needs the expert’s approval.”

## 2:10–2:40 · Present the actual evidence

**Show:** The comparison results, with all three conditions and the denominator visible. Caption: “Small synthetic test · AI decisions · One run per order.”

**Say:**
“In our small test, seven fictional orders needed an accept-or-reject decision. The handbook alone resolved one without asking the expert. The original expert answer resolved six. The approved note resolved seven.

None made a wrong accept-or-reject decision. One ambiguous case separates the answer and the note, so this does not prove the note is more accurate.”

## 2:40–2:55 · Close on the value

**Show:** Missing Monday name and: **Find the gap. Capture the answer. Test the handover.**

**Say:**
“The value is the complete handover: find the missing knowledge, get the expert’s approval, and test whether someone else can use it.

Find what only Morgan knows—while Morgan is still here to help.”

## Recording checks

- Clearly separate the personal opening from the fictional demo: Alder Works, its characters, orders and expert answer are demonstration material. This is not a real employee study or a demonstration of a Mercedes-Benz order.
- Use actual model responses. Never replace an unexpected answer with the desired one; record the failure and adjust the narration.
- Label shortened waits and replays. These time ranges guide the edit; they are not measured model response times.
- Keep the comparison caveat audible and visible. Requests for help are not wrong accept/reject decisions.
- Use the harder-order testing claim only if that implemented capability remains available. Do not imply a repair was demonstrated unless the recording shows it.
- Keep the personal story to the experience described. Do not add a lost client, financial loss or other outcome that has not been established.
